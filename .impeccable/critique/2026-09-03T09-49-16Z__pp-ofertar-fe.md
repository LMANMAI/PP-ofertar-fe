## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment (Assessment A):** Authored-for-this-product, with real conviction. Voseo is consistent in copy *voice*, not just vocabulary ("Escaneá tu primer ticket", "Guardamos tu interés"). `ComparePricesScreen` integrates real Argentine SEPA data with its own honest caveats. `FavoriteStoresScreen` hardcodes real chain brand colors (Carrefour navy, Dia red, Coto red, Jumbo green, Vea orange, Disco crimson, Changomas purple, La Anonima blue, Makro blue) so the map reads as this specific market. Most tellingly, PRODUCT.md's "no fabricar lo que no existe" principle is visibly load-bearing in the code: `PaymentMethodsScreen` tells the user outright there's no subscription yet; `rewards.ts` hardcodes "todavía no se puede canjear de verdad" into every reward's copy; `RedeemSuccessScreen` renames the confirmation moment "Interés guardado" instead of pretending a redemption happened. Unusual discipline for a project at this stage, and it reads as real authorship.

**Deterministic scan (Assessment B):** `detect.mjs --json` returned `[]` on `src`, `src/screens`, and `src/components` — consistent with this project's established pattern of the bundled detector having no real signal on React Native `StyleSheet`/accessibility props; treat as a null result, not a clean bill of health, since the manual scans below found real, file:line-verifiable issues in those same directories.

Concretely, the detector's manual-grep sweep corroborates Assessment A's consistency finding with hard evidence: `SmartShoppingListScreen.tsx:14` declares its own local `MIN_TRIPS_TO_BE_HABITUAL = 2` instead of importing the identical constant already exported from `src/components/forgottenProducts.ts` (and re-exported via `src/components/index.ts`) — the exact same "duplicate instead of reuse" habit Assessment A flagged qualitatively for store-badge initials logic (independently reimplemented in `TicketHistoryScreen`, `TicketDetailScreen`, `TicketProcessedScreen`). Two independent signals landing on the same root cause.

Theme-token adoption is otherwise excellent: **44/44 screens (100%)** use `useThemeColors()`, zero files import the static `colors` object directly. But two other token families are barely adopted: an exact-string-duplicate hex literal (`GoogleChooseAccountScreen.tsx:11`, `"#7DD4F5"` = `colors.cyan`) plus 22 `"#fff"`-shorthand occurrences across 10 files that happen to be color-equivalent to `colors.buttonText`/`colors.card` today but aren't wired to the tokens; and the `space` scale (`xs`/`sm`/`md`/`lg`/`xl`/`xxl`) is used only **13 times across 2 of 44 screens** (`AuthLoginScreen`, `AuthWelcomeScreen` — this session's layout pass) against 105 raw numeric spacing values in a 5-screen sample alone.

Accessibility labeling is broadly solid — 50 `accessibilityLabel` occurrences across 140 `Pressable` elements, and a full manual pass over every icon-only `Pressable` in the app found only **one** genuine gap (`CaptureTicketScreen.tsx:178-184`, the camera shutter button — no icon, no text, no label, on the app's most consequential single tap).

No browser injection/overlay step ran — this is a native RN app with no dev-server URL to visually inspect, consistent with every prior audit/critique pass this session.

## Overall Impression

This is a genuinely well-authored app for what it is — the honesty principle from PRODUCT.md is enforced in code, not just prose, and the core scan → savings loop has real craft (`ScanErrorScreen`, `RedeemSuccessScreen`). What's holding the score at Good rather than Excellent is a consistent tell across both assessments: the same idea gets reimplemented three times instead of shared once — store badge color, store initials, and now (per the detector) a shared constant. It's not a design-taste problem; it's a "the design system exists but isn't reached for" problem, visible in colors, spacing, and now business logic alike. The single biggest opportunity is closing that gap: route every store badge through the one `offerBadge()` helper, and treat `space`/hex-token adoption as a real pass rather than something only new screens happen to do.

## What's Working

- **The honesty principle is structural, not decorative.** Every screen touching an unbuilt feature (subscriptions, redemptions) says so plainly and consistently, in a genuinely warm way (`RedeemSuccessScreen`'s "Guardamos tu interés").
- **Error recovery is a standout.** `ScanErrorScreen` gives specific causes, actionable tips, a retry path, and a way to feel productive anyway — real care at a frustrating moment.
- **Theme-hook discipline is complete.** 44 of 44 screens use `useThemeColors()`; the dark-mode work from earlier this session fully stuck.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Help Center FAQ contradicts the app's own honesty principle.**
Why it matters: `HelpCenterScreen.tsx:17`'s FAQ #5 answer says points "se canjean por descuentos en tu próxima suscripción... o incluso un mes gratis" with no hedge — directly contradicting the "todavía no se puede canjear de verdad" framing maintained everywhere else (`rewards.ts:24-28`, `RewardDetailScreen`, `RedeemSuccessScreen`). A user who only reads the FAQ will believe they can buy a real discounted subscription today. This is the one place in the app where the honesty principle silently breaks.
Fix: Rewrite the FAQ answer to match the "preview"/"guardamos tu interés" language used consistently everywhere else.
Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify`

**[P1] Store identity (badge color + initials) is built three incompatible times, and the pattern repeats in logic too.**
Why it matters: `offerBadge()` (`offersApi.ts:210`, hash-derived) drives Home/Offers/OfferDetail; `TicketHistoryScreen` hardcodes blue (`#0D80CC`); `TicketDetailScreen`/`TicketProcessedScreen` hardcode red (`#E1352F`) regardless of which store the ticket is actually from. A user's own supermarket changes badge color depending which tab they're on. The detector independently found the same "duplicate instead of import" habit one level down in the code — `SmartShoppingListScreen.tsx:14` redeclares `MIN_TRIPS_TO_BE_HABITUAL` instead of importing the existing export — suggesting this is a recurring authoring habit, not a one-off.
Fix: Call the shared `offerBadge(storeName)` everywhere a store badge/initials render instead of three parallel implementations; import shared constants instead of redeclaring them.
Suggested command: `/impeccable audit`

**[P2] Design tokens exist but are inconsistently reached for — color and spacing alike.**
Why it matters: `#F8F9FB` and `#FDECE6` are hardcoded (not from `darkColors`) in `HomeScreen.tsx`, `MonthlyAnalysisScreen.tsx`, and `OffersScreen.tsx`, and will misrender as a pale out-of-place patch on a dark card — the exact bug class the earlier dark-theme sweep was meant to eliminate, reintroduced in newer screens. Separately, `GoogleChooseAccountScreen.tsx:11` hardcodes `"#7DD4F5"`, an exact duplicate of `colors.cyan`, that will silently drift out of sync if the token ever changes. And the `space` scale is used in just 2 of 44 screens (13 occurrences total) against well over 100 raw numeric spacing values elsewhere — the token exists, but "new screen" is currently the only trigger for using it.
Fix: Add theme-aware tokens for the two dark-mode-breaking fills and route them through `useThemeColors()`; replace the literal cyan with `colors.cyan`; adopt `space.xxx` in a real sweep, not just newly-touched screens.
Suggested command: `/impeccable colorize`, then `/impeccable layout`

**[P2] Ticket-discard confirmation breaks the app's visual language at its highest-anxiety moment.**
Why it matters: `TicketProcessedScreen.handleDiscard` (line 198) uses a bare native `Alert.alert`, while every other confirm-before-loss moment (`LogoutConfirmScreen`, `ConfirmRedeemScreen`) uses the app's bespoke navy-branded sheet. At the exact moment a user is about to lose scanned ticket data, the app suddenly looks like an unstyled OS dialog.
Fix: Reuse the existing confirm-sheet pattern for ticket discard instead of the native `Alert.alert`.
Suggested command: `/impeccable polish`

**[P2] Three screens show nothing (or misreport an error as "no results") when a successful fetch resolves empty.**
Why it matters: `TicketDetailScreen.tsx:118-131` and `MonthlyAnalysisScreen.tsx:110-123` both have `{loading && ...}`/`{error && ...}` branches but no `!ticket`/`!report` branch — if the fetch succeeds with a null result, the content area silently renders nothing. `TicketProcessedScreen.tsx`'s forgotten-products fetch has no `ActivityIndicator` at all, and its `.catch(() => setForgotten([]))` makes a genuine network error indistinguishable from "you have no forgotten products."
Fix: Add an explicit not-found `EmptyState` to both detail screens; give the forgotten-products fetch a real loading state and surface its error instead of swallowing it into an empty list.
Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

**[P3] `RecurringProductsScreen`'s expanded card overloads on open.**
Why it matters: Collapsed, the card is fine. Expanded, a single tap surfaces six distinct information blocks (price comparison, savings sentence, retailer promo label, "what you paid last time," other promotions with their own disclaimers, and other-brand alternatives) with only thin dividers between them — the densest screen in the app by a wide margin, and the working-memory rule caps useful simultaneous options at ~4.
Fix: Either paginate into a secondary "ver detalle completo" screen, or group into 2 clearly-headed sub-sections instead of one flat stack of six blocks.
Suggested command: `/impeccable distill`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer), overwhelmed by the scan flow:**
- `ScanMethodScreen` presents "Sacar fotos," "Adjuntar PDF," and "Escanear producto" as three equally-weighted cards with no framing of which one actually builds savings history — someone chasing instant gratification could default to barcode scanning and never discover the app's real core loop.
- Once a ticket uploads, the only way to learn it's still processing is returning to the Tickets tab manually — no push-notification hook, and Profile's only relevant toggle ("Alertas de ofertas") doesn't mention ticket-ready alerts.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User), checking savings one-handed leaving the store:**
- The center scan button in `BottomNav.tsx:86` uses the same `receipt-outline`/`receipt` glyph as the "Tickets" tab three positions to its right — under thumb-reach pressure right after checkout, two near-identical receipt icons in the same bar is a setup for a wrong tap.
- On the upside, `HomeScreen`'s savings hero card puts the number and a "Ver mis tickets" CTA in the first screenful, with its own dedicated retry state for spotty parking-lot connectivity.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User):**
- `RegisterStep1.tsx:228`'s required Terms checkbox uses a hardcoded `rgba(0,0,0,0.18)` border instead of `colors.border` — in dark mode, a near-black-alpha border on a dark card (`#111A2C`) is effectively invisible, and this is the one screen where missing it silently blocks account creation.
- The detector's manual accessibility sweep confirms this persona's concern with one more concrete instance: `CaptureTicketScreen.tsx:178-184`, the camera shutter button, has no icon, no text, and no `accessibilityLabel` — a screen-reader user gets zero announcement for the primary action on the capture screen.
- On the positive side, `AccessibilityInfo.isReduceMotionEnabled()` is genuinely checked before firing `LayoutAnimation`, and `accessibilityRole`/`accessibilityLabel`/`accessibilityState` are broadly present, including on the bottom-nav tabs — more careful than most apps at this stage.

## Questions to Consider

1. PRODUCT.md is explicit that ticket history should be the primary loop and price comparison secondary — but the app's most reachable action (the center scan button, present on every screen) opens a 3-way chooser giving barcode-lookup equal visual weight to ticket-scanning. Should the architecture make that hierarchy visible, with barcode-lookup demoted to a secondary entry point?
2. The points/rewards feature is five full screens in service of a mechanism that, by design, can only end in "we saved your interest." Would a single well-placed teaser card free up more of a first-time user's attention for the parts of the app that work today?
3. `RecurringProductsScreen`, `SmartShoppingListScreen`, and the Home carousel are three independently-built answers to "what do I buy again and where's it cheapest," each reimplementing its own price/discount/campaign formatting. Would users — and the codebase — be better served by one "your habits" surface with different views, rather than three parallel screens that can silently drift out of sync?
